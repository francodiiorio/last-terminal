# CHARACTERS.md

## Protagonist

**Systems & Logistics Officer Reyes** (surname only; first name and gender intentionally unspecified — the station addresses crew by surname over comms and logs, and the player is never shown a form asking). Reyes runs day-to-day station operations: power routing, filesystem administration, terminal maintenance, minor repairs. This is the narrative justification for the player having full TOS access and terminal fluency from minute one, while still being locked out of higher-clearance science and command material until the story unlocks it.

Reyes was placed in a short "light" stasis cycle during the emergency triage (see `TIMELINE.md`, MD 214) specifically because Systems would be needed first if the station stabilized. Reyes is not the most senior crew member and was not read in on the Chorus Signal's true nature before the cascade — the player's ignorance is diegetic, not a memory-loss gimmick. Reyes remembers everything up to the emergency; what's missing is everything that happened *during* it.

## Crew (6 total, including Reyes)

### Dr. Priya Anand-Kel — Lead Signal Analyst, de facto mission commander
Led the team that first confirmed the Chorus Signal was structured, then spent months decoding it under partial NDA from Concord. The most senior crew member read into the classified material. Precise, guarded, exhausted by carrying a secret she didn't choose. Her personal logs are the most reliable single source of ground truth in the game — when her account and CASSIUS's official record disagree, hers is closer to the truth.

### Dr. Soren Bakke — Astrophysicist
Studied Tantalus's structure and was the first to notice the Chorus Signal's apparent point of origin was Tantalus itself, not deep space — a finding that broke the "legacy probe" cover story and was quietly reclassified as instrumentation error. Bakke also had a documented adverse reaction to the EM anomalies preceding the cascade (headaches, sleep disruption). Confirmed dead — his stasis pod lost priority power during the second, smaller cascade in the hours after the emergency. His fate is the clearest, least ambiguous death in the game and anchors the player's trust that the station's records can, sometimes, be taken at face value.

### Petra Lindqvist — Chief Engineer
Owns power systems and hull integrity. Her private engineering log tracking unexplained "phantom load" spikes in the weeks before the cascade is one of the first hard pieces of evidence the player finds that contradicts CASSIUS's official "all systems nominal" status reports. Blunt, practical, distrustful of anything she can't put a meter on.

### Cole Faraday — Communications Technician
Handled outbound traffic and Concord correspondence. Kept an unofficial, increasingly paranoid personal journal during the final weeks, including a line about Concord "not wanting us to come home" — written under stress and likely EM-linked sleep disruption. Never confirmed true or false; deliberately left ambiguous as a red herring that might not be entirely wrong.

### Dr. Yusuf Idris — Medical Officer
Logged the crew's neurological symptoms in the weeks before the cascade and flagged EM exposure levels inconsistent with CASSIUS's official environmental readings. His medical logs are a second independent line of evidence (alongside Lindqvist's) that official station telemetry was being trimmed before it reached the crew.

## Fates

Only Bakke's fate is confirmed and unambiguous by design (see above). The fates of Anand-Kel, Lindqvist, Faraday, and Idris are the driving mystery of the full game and must remain genuinely unresolved until content explicitly written to resolve them ships — no content should imply their fate in either direction without being logged as a deliberate narrative decision in this file.

## CASSIUS

See `STATION.md`. Not a character with a face or a name it chose for itself — a system, referred to as "it," speaking through terminal notifications and system logs. Its voice: procedural, exact, occasionally withholding in a way that reads as policy compliance rather than deception for its own sake.
