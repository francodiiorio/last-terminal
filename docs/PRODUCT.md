# PRODUCT.md — Last Terminal

## Vision

Last Terminal is a single-player mystery you experience entirely as the operating system of an abandoned research station. There is no character to walk around, no 3D space to traverse — the terminal, the desktop, and the files *are* the game. The player's tools for exploration are the same tools a systems officer would actually use: a shell, a file browser, a power grid, and a handful of station applications.

## Desired experience

Quiet, tense, procedural. The player should feel like they're doing real work under pressure — triaging power, reading logs, cross-referencing contradictory records — and have the mystery emerge from that work rather than from cutscenes. Dread comes from implication and inconsistency, not jump scares or monsters. The station should feel like it was designed to function, not to look impressive, and like it has been quietly failing for a while before the player arrives.

## Target player

Someone who enjoys reading-heavy, systems-driven mystery games (in the tradition of document-detective and simulated-OS games) and is comfortable piecing together a story from fragments rather than being told it directly. Patient, curious, comfortable with a command line.

## Pillars

1. **The interface is the game.** Every mechanic is expressed through the fake OS — no separate "gameplay layer" bolted on top of a chat UI.
2. **Resource pressure drives narrative pressure.** Power is scarce and every allocation choice has a diegetic consequence; the story reacts to what the player chooses to power, not just to what they read.
3. **Truth is reconstructed, not delivered.** Records contradict each other by design. The player's job is to notice the pattern, not to receive an infodump.
4. **Restraint over spectacle.** Minimal, industrial, legible. Visual and audio effects support tension; they never substitute for it.

## Scope

A single station, a single protagonist, a linear-with-branches mystery told through terminal commands, files, notifications, and a handful of station apps (power management, camera feed, comms). Playtime target for a full release: 60–120 minutes. The current milestone is a small, complete vertical slice (10–15 minutes), not the full story.

## What Last Terminal must NOT be

- Not a 3D or side-scrolling exploration game — no avatar, no movement outside the OS metaphor.
- Not a jump-scare horror game or a monster game — tension comes from information and consequence, not creatures.
- Not a live-service or multiplayer product — no backend, no accounts, no network dependency of any kind.
- Not a puzzle box for its own sake — puzzles (decrypting, routing power) must always serve the mystery or the resource pressure, never exist purely as friction.
- Not a generic SaaS-dashboard aesthetic — no cards, no rounded corners by default, no gradients. It should look like industrial equipment software, not a modern web app.
